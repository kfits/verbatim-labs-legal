Closeout for Jira — Documentation

How to install and use Closeout for Jira.

Closeout turns a Jira project or epic — its data and its discussion — into a complete, governance-grade closure report, published straight to Confluence. It computes the hard numbers, drafts the narrative from your comments, asks you for anything Jira can't supply, and lets you review before it publishes.

What you need: a Jira Cloud site with Confluence, and Atlassian Rovo enabled. Closeout runs on Atlassian Forge and Rovo — nothing leaves your Atlassian cloud.

Installing

  1. Find Closeout for Jira on the Atlassian Marketplace and select Get it now (or install from the link shared with you).
  2. Choose the Jira site to install it on and confirm the requested permissions.
  3. Once installed, Closeout appears as a Closeout tab on your Jira projects, and as a Closeout panel on epics.

Generating a report for a whole project

1. Open the Closeout tab

Go to any Jira project and select the Closeout tab in the project navigation. Closeout automatically detects which project you're in.

2. Set your options

Choose the Confluence space to publish to, and decide whether to include a Project overview and Budget section — you can provide these yourself, or have Closeout draft them for your review. Add any extra context in the optional notes field.

3. Start the report

Select Start closure report. Closeout opens in Rovo chat, reads your project, and drafts the report. It will ask you for anything it needs (such as the budget figure or the project objective) — you can answer, or ask it to suggest a draft.

4. Review, then publish

Closeout shows you the full draft and asks whether to publish or change anything. Nothing is published until you confirm. Once you do, it creates a Confluence page and gives you the link — and, if you asked, posts a summary comment back on a Jira issue.

Generating a report for a single epic

Open an epic in Jira and use the Closeout panel on the issue. The flow is the same as above, but the report is scoped to that epic and its child issues rather than the whole project. (On non-epic issues, the panel explains that Closeout runs on epics and projects.)

What's in the report

Each report contains:

  • Project overview — objective and scope (you provide, or Closeout drafts).
  • Schedule performance — delivery against tracked due dates.
  • Budget performance — figures and variance (you provide, or mark not tracked).
  • Actuals against estimated hours — logged effort and coverage.
  • Quality performance — defects, reopens, and resolution times.
  • Risk performance — synthesised from the project's discussion, with issue references.
  • Lessons learned and recommendations — transferable takeaways, including what went well.
  • Project outcomes — what was delivered, drawn from the record.

Computed sections (schedule, actuals, quality) come straight from Jira data. Narrative sections (risk, lessons, outcomes) are synthesised from your project's comments and discussion. Closeout never invents a figure it can't know, and clearly flags anything that needs your input before sharing.

Good to know

  • Closeout produces a draft for you to finish — you review everything before it publishes.
  • Once published, the report is an ordinary Confluence page you can edit, share, or export.
  • Report quality improves with the quality of your project's discussion — the more context in your comments, the richer the narrative sections.
